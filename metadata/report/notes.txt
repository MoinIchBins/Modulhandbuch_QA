Questions sharing the same non-empty gold evidence set were assigned to the same split to reduce dependence between evaluation partitions. This constitutes a conservative splitting strategy, as the retrieval representations themselves were frozen and the complete chunk corpus remained available in all partitions. A consequence is increased split-level variance: large evidence groups are evaluated entirely within one partition, so differences in group difficulty can affect validation–test comparability.

7. Selector Performance Overview
## Selector Performance Overview

Across all selector types, **E5 consistently achieved the strongest performance**, followed by TF-IDF and Sentence-BERT. Pure **top-k selection** performed best with (k=1), reaching an overall F1 of **0.617** for E5, **0.551** for TF-IDF, and **0.374** for Sentence-BERT.

Pure **threshold selection** performed worse overall, mainly because it had to balance retrieval and abstention without limiting the number of selected chunks. Its best overall F1 scores were approximately **0.494** for E5, **0.487** for TF-IDF, and **0.358** for Sentence-BERT.

The combined **top-k + threshold** selector performed best overall. With (k=1) and representation-specific thresholds, E5 reached **0.647 F1**, TF-IDF **0.567**, and Sentence-BERT **0.399**. The improvement over pure top-1 selection shows that adding a threshold can improve performance by allowing the system to abstain on low-confidence questions while retaining the strong ranking behavior of top-1 retrieval.

8.1 finalist validation results
On the validation set, the **E5 + top-1 + threshold** configuration performed best, reaching a mean question-level F1 of **0.611** and an exact-match rate of **0.597**. Plain E5 top-1 followed closely with **0.597 F1**, while the relative-margin variant achieved **0.590 F1**. The thresholded E5 system therefore provided the best overall balance between correct retrieval and abstention, correctly rejecting **41.7%** of zero-gold questions. TF-IDF remained competitive but clearly weaker, with its best finalist reaching **0.500 F1**. Overall, the validation results confirmed both the advantage of the E5 representation and the benefit of combining top-1 retrieval with an absolute similarity threshold.


8.2 winner test result
On the held-out test set, the frozen **E5 + top-1 + threshold** configuration achieved a mean question-level F1 of **0.491** and an exact-match rate of **0.486**, both clearly below its validation performance. Precision and recall were similarly balanced at approximately **0.493** and **0.490**, while the system abstained on **20.8%** of questions and correctly abstained on **40.9%** of zero-gold cases. The size of the drop from validation to test suggests that the aggregate metrics alone are insufficient to explain the system’s behavior. A detailed analysis of the missed cases is therefore necessary to determine whether the errors stem primarily from retrieval failures, abstention decisions, properties of the test split, or limitations of the gold evidence mappings.

Manual error analysis revealed two important factors behind the lower test performance. First, several predictions that were penalized by exact chunk-set evaluation nevertheless contained sufficient evidence to answer the question, indicating that the gold mapping does not always capture all valid alternative evidence. Second, six test questions concerning the *Zugangsprüfung* belonged to the same unseen evidence group and were all retrieved incorrectly. Because questions sharing the same non-empty gold chunk set were intentionally kept within one split to prevent leakage, this cluster occurred only in the test set and contributed disproportionately to the performance drop.

correct numbers: A post-hoc sensitivity analysis yields a mean question-level F1 of approximately 0.586 when nine manually judged valid-alternative predictions are treated as correct and the eight zero-F1 questions belonging to the unseen Zugangsprüfung evidence group are excluded.

The strongest configuration decreased from **0.6474 F1 on development to 0.6111 on validation**, corresponding to a drop of **0.0363**. The original test F1 of **0.4907** over 144 questions corresponds to a total summed question-level F1 of (0.4907 \times 144 = 70.67). If the **9 manually identified alternative-valid evidence cases** are treated diagnostically as correct, this sum increases to (70.67 + 9 = 79.67). If the **6 Zugangsprüfung questions**, all of which contributed an F1 of 0, are additionally excluded, the adjusted diagnostic test F1 becomes (79.67 / (144-6) = 0.5773). The remaining validation-to-adjusted-test difference is therefore **0.6111 − 0.5773 = 0.0338**, which is slightly smaller than the **0.0363** decrease already observed from development to validation. This does not establish that the difference is statistically insignificant, but it suggests that much of the raw test drop from **0.6111 to 0.4907** can be attributed to identifiable properties of the evaluation data rather than a general collapse in retrieval performance.

because the split keeps questions sharing the same gold evidence together, performance is partly determined by which evidence groups land in each held-out split. Test contains several comparatively large evidence groups on which the frozen system performs very poorly.
see single gold only evaluations

Single-gold comparison. Restricting the evaluation to questions with exactly one gold chunk raises validation F1 from 0.611 to 0.655 and test F1 from 0.491 to 0.545. The validation–test gap therefore decreases only from about 0.120 to 0.111 F1, a reduction of roughly 0.010 points. Thus, differences in zero-gold and multi-gold composition explain only a small part of the test drop.
Difficult evidence groups. For descriptive analysis, a difficult group can be defined as an evidence group containing at least three questions for which the frozen system retrieves the correct gold chunk for less than half of the questions. The minimum group size avoids interpreting one or two individual errors as a systematic group-level problem, while the below-50% criterion means that failure is the dominant outcome within the group.

Group composition. Under this definition, validation contains 5 difficult groups covering 30 of 116 single-gold questions (25.9%), whereas test contains 7 difficult groups covering 40 of 112 questions (35.7%). Performance inside these groups is low in both splits—about 23.3% correct in validation and 15.0% in test. The Zugangsprüfung group is therefore only one part of a broader concentration of difficult evidence groups in the test split.

Interpretation of the remaining gap. Outside these difficult groups, performance is much closer: approximately 80.2% correct on validation versus 76.4% on test, a difference of only about 3.8 percentage points. This suggests that much of the larger aggregate validation–test difference is associated with the particular evidence groups assigned to each split. This is consistent with the leakage-aware splitting strategy: keeping questions with the same gold evidence together reduces leakage, but also increases split-to-split variation in evidence-group difficulty.
